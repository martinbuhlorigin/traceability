Why Finca Agua Sarca stands out

Finca Agua Sarca is defined by its terroir. Nestled in the hills of Dipilto at altitudes reaching 1,400 metres, the farm benefits from cool temperatures, sandy-loam soils, and steady rainfall that create ideal conditions for slow cherry development. The result is a cup with remarkable sweetness, structure, and clarity.

Owned by Isacio Javier Albir Vílchez, the farm has been in the Albir family for over 30 years — passed down through generations who have built an intimate knowledge of this land. At 67 hectares, with 56 under cultivation, Agua Sarca offers something rare: the scale to deliver consistent volume and the precision to produce award-winning micro-lots across multiple varieties.

Sustainability is central to Isacio's approach: native forest reserves are maintained across the estate, and the farm actively protects local water sources and wildlife habitats throughout the year.

Processing in detail — natural anaerobic
1

Selective hand-picking at peak ripeness

Only fully ripe cherries are harvested to ensure maximum sugar content. Underripe and overripe fruit is removed during field-level sorting.

2

Floating and sorting

Cherries are floated in clean water to eliminate impurities and any remaining defects before entering the fermentation stage.

3

Anaerobic fermentation — sealed, low-oxygen environment

Whole cherries are placed in sealed tanks with minimal oxygen exposure. This controlled environment builds concentrated sweetness, unique aromatics, and layered cup complexity without excessive fermentation.

4

Drying on African raised beds

After fermentation, cherries are dried as whole fruit on raised beds. Slow drying ensures even dehydration. Temperature and humidity are monitored throughout the process.

5

Resting and milling

Once the cherries reach ~12% humidity, the coffee rests before milling. This resting period allows flavors to stabilize and integrate before export.

About the region — Nueva Segovia, Nicaragua

Nueva Segovia is Nicaragua's northernmost coffee region, and arguably its most ambitious. Pressed against the Honduran border, its mountains reach elevations that force coffee trees to slow down — long maturation periods that build the layered sugars and acids that define the region's best cups.

Dipilto, where Agua Sarca is located, is the region's most elevated municipality. It is a place where established producers compete at international level and where Cup of Excellence results have repeatedly surprised global buyers with the depth and expressiveness of the coffees produced here. Isacio Albir is one of those producers — multiple finalist results since 2017 place him among the area's most respected names.

The combination of sandy-loam soils, consistent rainfall, and Dipilto's particular elevation band creates a distinct terroir — one that rewards the kind of careful, process-driven approach Agua Sarca has developed over three decades.
